Types of loft conversion

Rear dormer

A box dormer built out from the rear roof slope, giving full standing height across most of the room. A full-width rear dormer is the most popular loft in Bristol and usually fits a double bedroom and en-suite.

Small dormer

A narrower dormer for a smaller room or where you want to keep more of the original roof. Good value if you need one bedroom.

Hip-to-gable

On semi-detached and detached houses with a sloping hipped end, we build the end up into a straight gable wall and then add a rear dormer. This makes the biggest loft space, especially on the 1930s semis found across Henleaze, Horfield and Westbury-on-Trym.

Rooflight loft

Rooflights fitted into the existing roof slopes with no change to the roof shape. It is the cheapest option and rarely needs planning, but only works where the roof is already high enough.

What every loft conversion includes

Whatever the type, a loft conversion needs new floor joists and steel beams to carry the new floor, a proper staircase, fire protection to the escape route, insulation and building regulations sign-off. We include all of this in the price, so there are no surprises later.

Do you need planning permission for a loft conversion?

Many lofts are permitted development. You can add up to 40 m³ of roof space on a terraced house and 50 m³ on a semi-detached or detached house, dormers must face the rear, and they must sit back from the eaves. Mansards, front dormers and homes in conservation areas usually need planning permission. Our calculator estimates the added volume for your choices.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a loft conversion take?

Most dormer lofts take 7 to 9 weeks on site, and hip-to-gable lofts 8 to 12 weeks. Most of the work happens from scaffolding until the new stairs go in.

Is my loft suitable?

As a guide, you need around 2.2 m from the top of the ceiling joists to the underside of the ridge. We check this at the free site visit.

Do I need steel beams for a loft?

Almost always. The existing ceiling joists are not designed to be a floor, so steel beams and new joists carry the new room.

Do I need building regulations for a loft?

Yes. Every loft conversion needs building regulations approval for structure, fire safety, stairs and insulation.
