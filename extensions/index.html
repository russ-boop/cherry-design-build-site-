Types of extension we build

Rear extensions

The most popular project in Bristol. A single-storey rear extension usually creates a kitchen-diner or family room opening onto the garden, often with bi-fold or sliding doors and a roof lantern. We set the extension in 300 mm from each side wall as standard, which keeps the walls and gutters within your boundary.

Side extensions

Ideal on semi-detached, detached and end-of-terrace houses with space down the side. A side extension can create a utility room, downstairs WC, study or a wider kitchen. Single-storey side extensions can often be built under permitted development if they are no wider than half the original house.

Wrap-around extensions

A wrap-around combines a side and rear extension into one L-shaped space, giving the biggest open-plan room. Because it extends in two directions, it usually needs full planning permission.

Two-storey extensions

Add a bedroom or bathroom upstairs at the same time as the ground floor, for less than the cost of building twice. Two-storey extensions are more restricted under planning rules, so we check your house before design starts.

Do you need planning permission for an extension in Bristol?

Many single-storey rear extensions are permitted development: up to 3 m deep on a terraced or semi-detached house and 4 m on a detached house, and up to 4 m high. Under the larger home extension scheme you can go to 6 m and 8 m by applying for prior approval. Flats, listed buildings and homes in conservation areas such as Clifton, Cotham and Redland usually need full planning permission.

Every extension needs building regulations approval, and most terraced and semi-detached projects need a party wall notice. We handle all of this for you.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a single-storey extension take to build?

Most single-storey rear extensions take 10 to 14 weeks on site, after 4 to 8 weeks for drawings, approvals and party wall notices.

Why is the extension 300 mm in from the side walls?

Setting the extension in from each side keeps the new walls, foundations and gutters on your side of the boundary and avoids awkward junctions with the existing corners.

Can I stay at home during the build?

Usually, yes. We plan a temporary kitchen with you for the weeks when the back wall is opened up.

Do I need a party wall agreement?

If you build on or near a shared wall, cut into it for a steel beam or dig close to a neighbour's foundations, the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 applies. We serve the notices for you.
