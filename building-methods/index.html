Building methods for extensions in Bristol

Most people don't know how an extension is actually built. Here are the main methods we use across Bristol, and when each one makes sense.

Brick and block cavity wall

The standard UK method, and the most common in Bristol. An outer skin of brick or render, a cavity with insulation, and an inner skin of block. Solid, quiet and easy to match to most Bristol houses.

Best for: most rear and side extensions · Typical build: 10–14 weeks

Timber frame

A factory-made or site-built timber structure, insulated between the studs and finished in brick, render or cladding. Quicker to put up, less wet trades, and very good thermally.

Best for: faster builds, tight sites, clad finishes · Typical build: 8–12 weeks

Steel beams and openings

Not a wall type but part of almost every job. When we open the back or side of your house into the extension, a steel beam (RSJ) carries the house above, designed by a structural engineer.

Needed for: wider openings and open-plan layouts

Flat roofs

Usually EPDM rubber or GRP fibreglass over a warm-deck insulated roof. Clean and modern, and the best base for roof lanterns and rooflights.

Life span: 20–30+ years with good detailing

Pitched lean-to roofs

A sloping tiled or slate roof against the house, often with Velux windows. A traditional look that suits many Victorian and 1930s Bristol homes.

Best for: matching existing roofs and conservation areas

Choosing the right method

The right method depends on your house, the access to the back garden, your budget and the planning position. Brick and block is the default for most Bristol homes because it matches the existing house and is familiar to building control. Timber frame suits tight sites and faster programmes, and clad finishes.

We recommend a method at your free site visit and explain why. See it applied to extensions and loft conversions.

Frequently asked questions

Is timber frame as good as brick and block?

Yes, when designed and built properly. It is quicker and thermally very efficient, and can be finished in brick so it looks the same.

How long does a flat roof last?

A well-detailed EPDM or GRP warm-deck flat roof typically lasts 20 to 30 years or more.
